PFAS Removal with Advanced Oxidation Processes

Journal: International Journal of Environmental Analytical Chemistry, Volume 104 (2024), Issue 20 · DOI: 10.1080/03067319.2023.2225412
My contribution: Structured literature review, comparative synthesis, and figures.

Plain-English takeaway

Our review compares advanced oxidation processes for removing PFAS from water. We find boron-doped diamond (BDD) electro-oxidation consistently delivers strong removal for long-chain PFAS, and foam-fractionation combined with BDD performs especially well on complex leachates. Photochemical routes are generally limited for stubborn species like PFOS under typical conditions. We summarise mechanisms, typical efficiencies, and when each method is practical given energy and cost.

Key results at a glance

  • BDD electro-oxidation: high degradation for long-chain PFAS; robust but energy-intensive.
  • Foam fractionation → BDD: pre-concentrates PFAS, improving downstream destruction on leachates.
  • Photochemical AOPs: limited for PFOS/short-chain species without aggressive conditions or catalysts.
  • Practicality: trade-offs between removal efficiency, matrix effects, and operating cost/energy.

When to use what

ScenarioOften effectiveNotes
Complex leachate / high organic load Foam fractionation + BDD Pre-concentration reduces energy per mass removed; monitor foam stability.
Long-chain PFAS in treated streams BDD electro-oxidation High mineralisation potential; plan for power density and electrode lifespan.
Short-chain PFAS or PFOS-dominant Enhanced electrochemical routes Photochemical routes alone often underperform; consider catalysts or alternatives.

This table is a practitioner-oriented heuristic, not a substitute for site-specific piloting.
